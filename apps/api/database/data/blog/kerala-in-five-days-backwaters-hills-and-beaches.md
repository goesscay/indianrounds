Five days in Kerala is enough for one hill station, one backwater stay and one coastal stop, as long as you do not try to add more. A sensible order is Kochi, Munnar, Alleppey or Kumarakom, then a beach. The main rule is to keep driving days short and give the water its own unhurried day.

## How to shape the five days

Kerala is long and narrow, and the roads are winding, so distances that look small on a map take longer than expected. Plan on three bases rather than four or five. Moving every night is the most common reason people feel tired by day four.

A simple outline:

1. **Day 1:** Arrive in Kochi, explore Fort Kochi in the afternoon.
2. **Day 2:** Drive to Munnar through tea country, stay overnight.
3. **Day 3:** Munnar sightseeing, then drive towards the backwaters.
4. **Day 4:** Houseboat or lakeside stay at Alleppey or Kumarakom.
5. **Day 5:** Return to Kochi for your flight, or continue to Varkala or Kovalam if you have extra days.

If you can stretch to six or seven nights, add Thekkady for Periyar or a proper beach stay in Varkala.

## Kochi and Fort Kochi

Fort Kochi is a good gentle start. Walk past the Chinese fishing nets at the waterfront, look into St. Francis Church, wander the lanes of Jew Town and Mattancherry, and stop at one of the small cafes in old Portuguese and Dutch-era buildings. A Kathakali or Kalaripayattu performance in the evening is a popular introduction to local arts, though shows vary, so check timings with your hotel.

![Fort Kochi Fishing Nets](/photos/places/kerala/fort-kochi-fishing-nets.jpg)

Keep Day 1 light. Most flights arrive at different hours, and the old town is best enjoyed at a slow walking pace.

## Munnar: tea, hills and mist

The road from Kochi to Munnar usually takes roughly four to five hours depending on traffic and stops. The scenery changes as you climb, with spice-scented air, small waterfalls and, finally, tea plantations that roll across the hills.

![Eravikulam Munnar](/photos/places/kerala/eravikulam-munnar.jpg)

In Munnar, plan a morning at the tea gardens and a visit to a tea museum or factory, if open. Eravikulam National Park is known for the Nilgiri tahr, but access and rules change by season, so check the current status before you plan around it. Evenings are cool even in warmer months, so carry a light layer.

Do not overfill the day. A viewpoint, a plantation walk and a long lunch is plenty.

## Alleppey and Kumarakom: the backwaters

Kerala's backwaters are a network of canals, lakes and rivers lined with coconut palms and small villages. Alleppey is the better-known base for houseboats, while Kumarakom, on Vembanad Lake, is quieter and suits travellers who prefer a lakeside stay with birdwatching.

A houseboat night is a highlight for many. Cruising usually runs through the day, with a halt for the night. Expect simple, freshly cooked Kerala meals on board, often with local fish. Check what is included before you book, and ask how many boats share the route in peak season.

> **Tip:** If you only have one backwater night, choose a smaller boat or a lakeside homestay away from the busiest Alleppey stretch. You get more quiet and a better sense of village life.

For more on what to eat on this route, see our guide to [where to eat in Kerala](/blog/where-to-eat-in-kerala).

## A beach to finish: Varkala or Kovalam

If you have an extra day or two, end with the coast. Varkala has a cliff-top walkway with views of the Arabian Sea, small cafes and a calmer feel. Kovalam has a lighthouse beach and is closer to Trivandrum airport, which makes it practical if you are flying out from there.

![Kovalam Lighthouse Beach](/photos/places/kerala/kovalam-lighthouse-beach.jpg)

Swim only where lifeguards and flags indicate it is safe, as currents can be strong on parts of this coast, especially during the monsoon.

## Best time and what to pack

October to March is the most comfortable window for most travellers, with lower humidity and pleasant evenings in the hills. The monsoon, roughly June to September, brings lush greenery and lower rates, and some travellers like it for slow, rainy stays, but boat services and hill roads may be affected. If you are comparing seasons across India, our [month-by-month guide](/blog/best-time-to-visit-india-month-by-month) helps.

Pack light cotton clothes, a rain jacket, mosquito repellent, comfortable sandals and a warm layer for Munnar. Dress modestly at temples, where some require traditional attire.

### Getting around

Private cars with drivers are the easiest way to link Kochi, Munnar and the backwaters. Trains and state buses are available too, but they suit longer trips better than a tight five days. Flights into Kochi are well connected from Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai and Delhi. Always confirm current schedules and road conditions before you travel.
