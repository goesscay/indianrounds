Choose India if you have a short break, want little paperwork and prefer a relaxed pace. Choose abroad if you have about a week or more, want something new for both of you and are happy to handle a passport and a visa. Neither is the better honeymoon; the right one is the one that fits your leave, season and mood.

Here is a practical way to compare the two, followed by a few pairings to start your shortlist.

## Budget: style matters more than the country

A honeymoon abroad is not automatically more expensive, and India is not automatically cheap. What changes the cost is how you travel: the hotel category, private cars or shared transfers, how many flights you take and how long you stay.

![Alleppey Houseboat](/photos/places/kerala/alleppey-houseboat.jpg)

Within India, a houseboat night or a palace stay can cost as much as a good resort overseas. Abroad, flights and visa fees are extra costs that an India trip does not have. Compare the same style of stay in both places rather than the destination alone, and check current prices before you decide.

## Travel time and leave

India usually wins when your leave is short. A direct flight or an overnight train can put you on a beach or a hill station by lunchtime, so a five-day trip feels like a full honeymoon.

Overseas trips need a little more room. Some places are only a few hours away by air, but you still lose time at airports and on arrival day. As a rough guide, many couples feel a week works well for a single overseas destination, while two countries or a long-haul trip need more. If you can take only a few days, India is the easier choice.

## Season: pick the weather, then the place

The best months differ sharply from place to place. The Kerala backwaters and hills are usually at their most comfortable in the cooler months, while the Kashmir valley is popular in spring and summer and has a snowy appeal in winter. Bali and Thailand have wet and dry seasons that shift with the coast you pick.

![Kathakali Kerala](/photos/places/kerala/kathakali-kerala.jpg)

Your wedding date often decides the month, so choose the destination that suits that month rather than the other way round. Our guide to the [best time to visit India month by month](/blog/best-time-to-visit-india-month-by-month) is a good starting point, and each [destination page](/explore-the-world) shows its busy and quiet seasons.

## Paperwork and planning

Travelling within India needs no passport or visa, which keeps planning light and last-minute changes easy. Overseas trips need a valid passport with enough validity left, and many destinations need a visa or an online authorisation. Entry rules change from time to time, so always confirm with the official source or ask us before you book. Our note on [visa basics for Indian travellers](/blog/visa-basics-for-indian-travellers-how-to-check) explains how to check.

Newly married couples should also make sure the names on tickets, passports and bookings match. If your passport is being updated after the wedding, allow time for it before you travel.

## Privacy and pace

A honeymoon is about time together, so think about how much space you want. Houseboats, villas and small riads give you a lot of privacy. Busy city hotels and crowded sightseeing routes give you less.

Pace matters just as much. Some couples want a lazy beach week; others enjoy a new place every two days. A good rule is to keep one long stay rather than many short ones, and to leave a free half-day in each place.

## A simple way to choose

Ask yourselves these five questions in order:

1. How many days of leave do we really have?
2. Which month are we travelling?
3. Do we both have valid passports, and are we happy to arrange a visa?
4. Do we want quiet and slow, or active and varied?
5. What is the one memory we want from this trip?

If your answers point to short, easy and quiet, start with India. If they point to longer, new and varied, look overseas.

> **Tip:** Write down your leave days and travel month before you open a single brochure. Those two facts remove half the options straight away.

## Six pairings to get you started

- **Kerala backwaters, if you want calm.** Slow houseboat days and green hills suit couples who like to unwind. See [Kerala](/destinations/kerala) and our [Kerala in five days](/blog/kerala-in-five-days-backwaters-hills-and-beaches) plan.
- **Kashmir, if you want scenery without a passport.** Lakes, gardens and mountains in one trip. Read [when to visit Kashmir](/blog/best-time-to-visit-kashmir) before you pick dates.
- **Rajasthan, if you want romance and history.** Palaces, lakes and desert evenings. See [Rajasthan](/destinations/rajasthan) and the [seven-day plan](/blog/rajasthan-in-seven-days-forts-palaces-and-desert).
- **Bali, if you want beach and culture together.** A good fit for a week or more. Our [Bali first-timer's guide](/blog/bali-first-timers-guide-where-to-stay-and-how-long) helps you choose where to stay.
- **Thailand, if you want variety on one trip.** City, islands and the north in one route. See [Thailand](/destinations/thailand).
- **Morocco, if you want something different.** Blue towns, old medinas and desert nights, best for couples who enjoy exploring. See [Morocco](/destinations/morocco).

To make the final call, pick two options, one in India and one abroad, and compare them on days, month, paperwork and budget. The answer is usually obvious once the numbers are side by side.
