Azerbaijan is best seen in five to six nights: two or three in Baku for the old city and modern skyline, a day for fire temples and rock art nearby, and two more for the green foothills around Sheki and Gabala. It is a little-known country with a strong sense of place, and it suits travellers who like cities with history and easy day trips.

## Why Azerbaijan

Azerbaijan sits where Europe and Asia meet, on the Caspian Sea. You can walk a medieval walled city in the morning and stand beneath a glass-and-steel tower in the afternoon. Beyond Baku, the landscape shifts quickly to forested hills, high villages and wide plains.

## Baku: the old and the new

Plan two to three nights in the capital.

![Flame Towers Baku](/photos/places/azerbaijan/flame-towers-baku.jpg)

The **Old City**, or Icherisheher, is a walled quarter of narrow lanes, caravanserais and mosques. The **Maiden Tower** and the **Palace of the Shirvanshahs** are the best-known sights, and the lanes are best explored on foot without a plan.

From there, walk to the **Baku Boulevard**, a long waterfront promenade along the Caspian. In the evenings, the **Flame Towers** are lit up in moving patterns and are visible from much of the city.

The **Heydar Aliyev Centre**, designed by Zaha Hadid, is worth seeing for its flowing, curved shape even if you do not go inside. It is a short drive from the centre.

> **Tip:** Visit the Old City in the early morning or late evening. Day tours crowd the main lanes between late morning and mid-afternoon.

## Beyond Baku: Gobustan, Sheki and Gabala

### Gobustan and the fire sites

South of Baku, **Gobustan** has thousands of ancient rock carvings, and a small museum helps put them in context. Nearby, small mud volcanoes bubble in an unusual, almost lunar landscape. Many travellers include these on the same day.

![Sheki Khan Palace](/photos/places/azerbaijan/sheki-khan-palace.jpg)

On the Absheron Peninsula, northeast of Baku, two sites show why Azerbaijan is called the Land of Fire:

- **Ateshgah**, a temple complex linked to fire worship, with a historic Indian connection through merchants who came along trade routes
- **Yanar Dag**, a hillside where natural gas burns continuously from the rock

Many Indian visitors are interested in the Ateshgah link, so ask your guide to explain it on site. A half-day usually covers both.

### Sheki: a palace and a mountain town

Sheki is roughly a long day's drive from Baku, so it works better with an overnight. The highlight is the **Sheki Khan's Palace**, a small summer palace covered in coloured glass windows and painted interior walls. The old caravanserai nearby is now a hotel, and the town is known for its sweets, especially a layered, honeyed pastry.

The drive passes rolling hills, orchards and small villages. If you start early, you can stop along the way for tea and local food.

### Gabala: lakes and greenery

**Gabala** is a mountain resort region that suits families and couples. There is a lake called **Nohur** with walking paths and boat rides, and a cable car and viewpoints in the surrounding hills. In summer it is cool and green. In winter, ski areas operate in the area.

It can be combined with Sheki on a two-night loop out of Baku, or visited on its own as a longer day trip if you start early.

## Suggested plan

A simple six-night route could look like this:

1. **Nights 1 to 3:** Baku, with one half-day for Absheron and one for Gobustan.
2. **Night 4:** Drive to Gabala via Shamakhi, with stops.
3. **Night 5:** Sheki, palace and bazaar.
4. **Night 6:** Return to Baku and fly out.

If you want a shorter trip, skip Sheki and use Gabala as a single day trip.

## Food and everyday life

Azerbaijani food will feel comfortable to many Indian travellers, with plov (rice with saffron, dried fruit and sometimes meat), flatbreads, kebabs and a lot of tea served in small glasses. **Dolma**, **dushbara** (small dumplings in broth) and vegetable-and-egg dishes are widely available. Vegetarians should ask for dishes without meat stock.

Most people in Baku are used to visitors, and English is commonly spoken in tourist places. Outside the capital, a driver or guide helps a great deal.

## Practical points

- **Currency:** Check current exchange rates; cards are widely accepted in Baku, but carry some cash for villages.
- **Dress:** Cover shoulders and knees at mosques, and remove shoes where asked.
- **Roads:** Good main roads, slower on mountain stretches. A private driver is the easiest option.
- **Tours:** Hotels and guides can arrange day trips, but we suggest booking transfers in advance in peak months.
