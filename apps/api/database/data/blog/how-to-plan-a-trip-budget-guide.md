To plan a trip budget, list every category of cost, estimate each one using current prices from reliable sources, add a buffer, and then divide the total by the number of travellers. Working in rupees from the start keeps the numbers honest, because exchange rates and card charges can quietly add up.

This guide walks through that process step by step, so you can decide what you can afford before you fall in love with a destination.

## Decide who is paying for what

Before you open any booking site, settle three things: the total you are comfortable spending, the dates you can travel, and the number of people sharing costs. A budget that does not name the number of travellers is not much use, because some costs are shared (a hotel room, a taxi) and some are per person (flights, entry tickets).

Write down your must-haves and nice-to-haves separately. A must-have might be a direct flight; a nice-to-have might be a spa day. When money gets tight, the second list shrinks first.

## The main cost categories

Almost every trip fits into the same handful of categories.

![Phi Phi Islands, Thailand](/photos/places/thailand/phi-phi-islands.jpg)

- **Flights and transfers:** your return fare from your home city, plus any internal flights or trains
- **Stay:** hotels, homestays or hostels, including taxes and any resort or city fees
- **Food:** breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks, which depend heavily on where you eat
- **Local transport:** metro, cabs, buses, rentals and airport transfers
- **Activities and entry tickets:** attractions, tours, experiences and guides
- **Visa and documents:** visa fees, passport renewal if needed, photographs and forms
- **Insurance:** a travel insurance policy for the whole trip
- **Shopping and souvenirs:** keep this as a fixed amount rather than an open question
- **Connectivity:** a local SIM, eSIM or international roaming pack

Prices for all of these change with season and demand, so always check current rates before you commit. Do not rely on a figure you saw a year ago.

## How to estimate each category

Start with the big items, since they decide the rest. Check fares across a few flexible dates, because a one or two day shift can change the price meaningfully. For stay, shortlist three or four options in different price bands and note the total including taxes, not just the headline rate.

For food and local transport, estimate a daily amount per person and multiply by the number of days. If you are unsure, read recent traveller forums or official tourism websites for a sense of everyday costs, then choose a figure toward the upper end to stay safe. Eating at local places, using public transport and cooking occasionally if your stay has a kitchen are all well-known ways to keep daily spend down.

For activities, list the three or four things you truly want to do and look up their current prices. Then leave a small amount for the things you did not plan.

## Do not forget the hidden costs

These are the items that most often break a budget, because people forget them until they arrive.

![Uluwatu Temple, Bali](/photos/places/indonesia/uluwatu-temple.jpg)

1. Foreign transaction and currency conversion charges on cards
2. Airport transfers at both ends, sometimes at odd hours
3. Baggage fees on low-cost airlines
4. Tips and service charges in countries where they are expected
5. Visa processing, courier or appointment fees
6. Cab fares to and from your own airport at home
7. Laundry, SIM cards, and small daily extras

Add these up as a separate line so you can see them. Ask your bank or forex provider about current charges, since they differ from one provider to another.

> **Tip:** Add a buffer of roughly ten to fifteen percent on top of your total estimate, and treat it as untouchable unless something genuinely unexpected happens. If you return with it unspent, it becomes your next trip's start.

## Where to save without spoiling the trip

Saving works best when you cut the things you will not miss. Booking flights and hotels early, travelling in the shoulder season, and choosing a central stay that reduces cab costs often matter more than skimping on meals.

Compare a fully independent plan with a packaged one. A group departure may bundle stays, transfers and sightseeing into a single plan, which can make the total easier to predict, though you should always read exactly what is included before you book. ## Track the budget before and during the trip

Create a simple sheet with each category, your estimate, and the actual amount. Update it as you book, so you can see early if you are going over. During the trip, note spending at the end of each day. It takes two minutes and prevents a nasty surprise on the flight home.

Keep every booking confirmation, and check the cancellation and refund terms of each booking before you pay, as they differ widely.

## A simple budgeting routine

1. Choose your dates and the number of travellers
2. List the categories above and add current prices for each
3. Add the hidden costs line and the buffer
4. Divide by the number of travellers to get a per-person figure
5. Compare it with what you can spend and adjust the trip, not the maths

If the number is higher than you hoped, change the length of the trip, the season or the destination before you change the quality of the plan. A shorter trip done well usually beats a longer trip done in a rush.
