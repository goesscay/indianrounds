Seven days is enough to see four of Rajasthan's best cities if you pick the route carefully. A good loop is Jaipur, Jodhpur, Jaisalmer and Udaipur, with long drives kept to a minimum. Fly into one city and out of another to avoid doubling back.

## Planning the route

Rajasthan is large, and road distances between the big cities are measured in several hours, not minutes. Trying to see Bikaner, Pushkar, Ranthambore and all of the above in one week usually means more time in the car than at the sights. Four bases is the practical limit for seven days.

A route that works well:

1. **Days 1 to 2:** Jaipur
2. **Day 3:** Drive to Jodhpur
3. **Days 4 to 5:** Jaisalmer, with a desert evening
4. **Days 6 to 7:** Udaipur, then fly out

Flying into Jaipur and out of Udaipur is a common pairing. Check current flight options from Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Chennai before you finalise anything.

## Jaipur: the Pink City

Start with Amber Fort in the morning, when it is quieter, and allow a couple of hours to explore the courtyards, mirror work and views. Later, visit the City Palace and Jantar Mantar, then stop at the Hawa Mahal facade for a photo. Evenings are good for the old city bazaars, where you can browse block-printed textiles, jewellery and blue pottery.

![Hawa Mahal Jaipur](/photos/places/rajasthan/hawa-mahal-jaipur.jpg)

If you enjoy shopping, our guide on [what to buy in Rajasthan](/blog/what-to-buy-in-rajasthan) covers the essentials. For food, try dal baati churma, pyaaz kachori and lassi at a busy local shop rather than only at hotels.

## Jodhpur: the blue city

Jodhpur is dominated by Mehrangarh Fort, which sits high above the city. The audio guide is useful, and the museum inside is well regarded. Afterwards, walk down into the old city, where many houses are painted in shades of blue, and visit the Sardar Market and clock tower for spices and sweets.

![Blue City Jodhpur](/photos/places/rajasthan/blue-city-jodhpur.jpg)

The drive from Jaipur typically takes five to six hours with a stop. If you prefer to break it, Ajmer and Pushkar are possible stops, though they add time.

## Jaisalmer: the golden fort

Jaisalmer Fort is unusual because people still live inside it. Stay within walking distance of the fort if you can, and explore the narrow lanes, Jain temples and havelis such as Patwon Ki Haveli.

![Sam Sand Dunes Jaisalmer](/photos/places/rajasthan/sam-sand-dunes-jaisalmer.jpg)

The classic desert experience is an evening at the Sam sand dunes, with a camel ride, folk music and a campfire dinner. Some travellers find the busiest dune areas touristy, so ask about quieter camps or less crowded stretches. Nights in winter can be cold, so carry a jacket.

> **Tip:** Jodhpur to Jaisalmer is a long road day. Start early, break the journey for lunch, and avoid driving after dark where possible, as livestock on highways can be a hazard.

## Udaipur: lakes and palaces

Udaipur feels very different from the dry west. The City Palace complex looks over Lake Pichola, and a short boat ride at sunset is a relaxing end to your trip. Walk through the lanes near the lake for miniature paintings, silver work and cafes with rooftop views.

If you have a spare half day, Ranakpur's marble Jain temple and the Kumbhalgarh area are well-known excursions from Udaipur, though they need extra driving time and an early start. Confirm current visiting hours before going.

## Practical tips

- **Season:** October to March is the most comfortable period. Summers are very hot, especially in the desert west. Monsoon months can be green and quieter but humid. See our [month-by-month guide](/blog/best-time-to-visit-india-month-by-month) for a broader view.
- **Transport:** A private car with driver is the simplest way to link the cities. Trains are an option on some routes, but timings and availability vary, so check current schedules.
- **Clothing:** Light cotton in the day, a warm layer in winter evenings, and a scarf or cap for sun. Cover shoulders and knees at temples.
- **Money:** Carry some cash for markets, camel rides and small tips. Agree on prices before any ride or guide.
- **Entry tickets:** Many forts and palaces charge separate fees for entry, cameras and audio guides. Check current prices on arrival.

### If you have more time

Add two or three nights for Pushkar and Bikaner, or Ranthambore if you are keen on a wildlife safari. Tiger sightings are never guaranteed, and national park rules and zones change, so treat it as a bonus rather than the main reason for the trip.

If you have fewer days, drop Jaisalmer and keep Jaipur, Jodhpur and Udaipur. That saves the longest drive and still gives a good picture of the region.

## Choosing how to travel

Rajasthan suits first-time travellers because the major sights are well established, and there is plenty of choice for stays, from simple hotels to restored havelis.
