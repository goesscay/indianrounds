Four to five nights is the right length for a first trip to Dubai. It gives you time for the modern skyline, the old creek district, a desert evening and a beach day, with one slower day for shopping or rest. Most visitors from India find the flight short and the city easy to enjoy, since Hindi and English are widely understood.

Dubai is a city of contrasts, with glass towers a short drive from old trading houses, and the best trips make room for both. It is also hot for much of the year, so your dates will shape what you do.

## When to go

The most comfortable months are roughly November to March, when days are warm and evenings are pleasant. This is also the busiest and most expensive period. Summers are very hot and humid, and most people spend them indoors in malls and air-conditioned attractions. If you travel in the hotter months, plan outdoor activities for early morning or after sunset, and expect lower hotel rates.

## The skyline: Downtown and the Burj Khalifa

Downtown Dubai is where the tallest tower in the world rises over Dubai Mall and the Dubai Fountain. Book an observation deck visit in advance for a time close to sunset, and check current ticket options and rules before you go. In the evening, the fountain shows run on a schedule, and the walkways around the lake are a pleasant place to stroll.

## Old Dubai: creek, souks and abras

Cross to the older side of the city for a very different feel. Take a traditional wooden abra across Dubai Creek for a very small fare, then wander through the spice and gold souks in Deira. The Al Fahidi historic district has narrow lanes, wind towers and small museums. Bargaining is common in the souks, so ask for a price, smile and go slowly.

![Dubai Creek Souk](/photos/places/united-arab-emirates/dubai-creek-souk.jpg)

## Beaches, the Marina and the Palm

Dubai's coast has several public beaches, including Jumeirah and the area around JBR, where you can swim and walk on the promenade. Dubai Marina has a lively waterfront with restaurants and the tram. The Palm Jumeirah is the famous man-made island, with beach clubs and resorts, and you can see its shape from above by choosing a viewing deck on the Palm or a boat or helicopter ride.

The Dubai Frame is a quick stop that gives views of both old and new Dubai.

## Desert evenings

A desert safari is a popular part of a first trip. Most include dune driving, a camel ride, a sunset stop and a barbecue dinner with performances in a camp. If you are prone to car sickness, tell the operator and choose a gentler option, and children and older travellers may prefer a calmer evening camp over a dune-bashing ride.

![Desert Safari](/photos/places/united-arab-emirates/desert-safari.jpg)

> **Tip:** Book big-ticket attractions, such as the observation deck and the desert safari, a few days ahead in the busy winter months, and keep your most flexible activities for the last day.

## A simple 4-night plan

1. **Day 1:** Arrive, settle in, an easy evening walk along the Marina or Jumeirah.
2. **Day 2:** Old Dubai in the morning, the Dubai Frame and a souk visit, then the Downtown fountain in the evening.
3. **Day 3:** Beach time and the Palm, then dinner at the Marina.
4. **Day 4:** Late morning rest, then an afternoon desert safari.
5. **Day 5:** Last-minute shopping and departure.

If you have an extra day, many visitors add Abu Dhabi, around an hour and a half's drive away. The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and the Louvre Abu Dhabi are the highlights, and both are covered on our [UAE page](/destinations/united-arab-emirates).

## Where to stay and practical points

Downtown suits first-timers who want to see the skyline, while the Marina and JBR are good for beach access and restaurants. Deira and Bur Dubai are more budget-friendly and close to the old town. The metro connects many main areas, which helps keep taxi costs in check.

- Entry rules and visa processes for Indian passport holders can change, so check the current rules with the official source or with us before you book.
- Dress codes are relaxed in tourist areas, but modest clothing is expected in mosques and older neighbourhoods.
- Public behaviour rules in the UAE are stricter than many visitors expect, so read up before you travel.
- Carry some local currency for taxis and souks, and check card charges.
