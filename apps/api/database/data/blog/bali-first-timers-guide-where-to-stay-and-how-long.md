Most first-time visitors from India are happiest with six to eight nights in Bali, split between two bases: Ubud for culture and greenery, and a coastal area such as Seminyak or Uluwatu for beaches. Staying in one place for the whole trip means long drives every day, and moving more than twice eats into your holiday.

Bali is smaller than it looks on a map, but roads are narrow and traffic is heavy, especially around Canggu, Seminyak and Kuta. A trip that looks easy on paper can mean an hour or more in a car between two points that are only a few kilometres apart. That one fact should shape how you choose where to stay.

## How many nights do you need?

- **4 to 5 nights:** Enough for one base and a few day trips. You will see the highlights but feel rushed.
- **6 to 8 nights:** The comfortable range for two bases and a slower pace.
- **9 to 11 nights:** Lets you add Nusa Penida, a Gili island or a quiet east-coast stay.

Flights from India usually take one stop, and many arrive late at night or early morning, so your first day may be half lost. Add a night if you want a proper first day.

## Ubud: culture, rice fields and slow mornings

Ubud sits in the green hills inland, and it is where most people find the Bali they imagined. You will find art galleries, yoga studios, small warungs and easy access to the Tegalalang rice terraces, Tirta Empul and the Monkey Forest. It is also a good base for the Mount Batur sunrise walk.

![Bali Swing Ubud](/photos/places/indonesia/bali-swing-ubud.jpg)

The trade-off is that there is no beach, and the centre can get busy in the afternoon. Many travellers prefer a stay slightly outside the centre, in a villa with a pool and a view of the paddies, and take a short ride into town for dinner.

## Seminyak: beaches, restaurants and sunsets

Seminyak is the polished beach side, with a long stretch of sand, boutique shops and a strong dining scene. It suits couples and friends who want cafes, shopping and sunset drinks within easy reach. Nearby Kuta is louder and cheaper, with a younger, party feel, and the airport is close, which helps on arrival and departure days.

![Seminyak Beach Sunset](/photos/places/indonesia/seminyak-beach-sunset.jpg)

Seminyak is not the place for a peaceful, empty beach. Think of it as the convenient, comfortable option rather than the quiet one.

## Canggu: surf, cafes and a younger crowd

North of Seminyak, Canggu draws surfers, remote workers and café hoppers. It has a relaxed, informal mood, but traffic is one of its known frustrations. It tends to suit solo travellers and groups of friends more than families with older relatives.

## Uluwatu and the Bukit peninsula: cliffs and quiet

On the southern tip, Uluwatu offers dramatic cliffs, clifftop villas, surf breaks and the famous sea temple. Beaches here are often reached down steps or paths, so they can be less convenient for small children or anyone with mobility concerns. It is a lovely area for honeymooners who want calm and views.

## A simple two-base plan

> **Tip:** Book your Ubud nights first and your beach nights at the end. You will be tired after sightseeing, and a relaxed final stretch near the airport makes departure far easier.

A tried-and-tested pattern looks like this:

1. Arrive in Denpasar and transfer to Ubud for 3 or 4 nights.
2. Take day trips to the terraces, the water temple and Mount Batur.
3. Move to Seminyak or Uluwatu for 3 or 4 nights.
4. Add Tanah Lot and Uluwatu temple at sunset, with a free day to rest.

If you are travelling with children or grandparents, keep the same structure but reduce long days out, and ask for ground-floor rooms or villas with fewer steps.

## Getting around and practical points

Most visitors hire a private car and driver for day trips, which is often practical and reasonably priced when split between a few people. App-based cars and bikes are available in some areas, though pick-up rules in certain zones can be restrictive. Scooter hire is popular, but it requires the right licence and insurance, and many visitors choose not to risk it on busy roads.
