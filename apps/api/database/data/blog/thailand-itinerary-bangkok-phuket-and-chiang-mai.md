Bangkok, Phuket and Chiang Mai make a very good first trip to Thailand, and eight to ten nights is enough to do all three without rushing. Spend three nights in Bangkok, four on Phuket and its islands, and two or three in Chiang Mai, using short domestic flights to link them.

Each place offers something different: Bangkok has palaces, markets and food, Phuket has sea and sand, and Chiang Mai has temples, mountains and a gentler pace. The order matters because of how flights work, so we will cover that first.

## Choosing your route

Indian travellers can usually find direct flights to Bangkok from several metros, and some routes to Phuket as well, though schedules change, so check what is currently operating. The cleanest plan is often an open-jaw: fly into Bangkok, then fly out of Phuket (or the reverse), so you never backtrack.

![Phuket Old Town](/photos/places/thailand/phuket-old-town.jpg)

A typical order is:

1. Bangkok for 3 nights
2. Chiang Mai for 2 to 3 nights (a short domestic flight north)
3. Phuket for 4 nights (a domestic flight south, often via Bangkok)
4. Fly home from Phuket

Putting the beach last gives you a restful finish. If you are travelling with older relatives, you may prefer to drop Chiang Mai and spend more time in Bangkok and Phuket.

## Bangkok: temples, rivers and food

Start with the Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew, which need modest dress, so cover shoulders and knees. Then see Wat Pho with its reclining Buddha and cross the river by ferry to Wat Arun. Allow a half day, and go early before the heat builds.

![Grand Palace Bangkok](/photos/places/thailand/grand-palace-bangkok.jpg)

The rest is about exploring. Take a longboat or river ferry, wander Chinatown's Yaowarat Road in the evening for street food, and visit a big weekend market if your dates allow. For shopping, Bangkok's large malls and night markets suit different budgets. The BTS Skytrain and MRT metro are the easiest ways to dodge the traffic.

If you want a day trip, the floating markets and the old capital of Ayutthaya are both popular, though they take most of a day. Our guide to [street food in Bangkok and Phuket](/blog/street-food-in-bangkok-and-phuket) covers what to try.

## Chiang Mai: temples, hills and slower days

Chiang Mai's old city is walled and moated, filled with temples and small cafes. Wat Phra That Doi Suthep sits on a hill above the city and is reached by a long staircase or a short ride up. Visit in the morning for clearer views.

![Elephant Sanctuary Chiang Mai](/photos/places/thailand/elephant-sanctuary-chiang-mai.jpg)

Another popular activity is time at an ethical elephant sanctuary. Choose operators that keep a no-riding, hands-off approach, and ask how they treat their animals before you book. Evenings are good for the night markets and a bowl of khao soi, the curry noodle soup of the north.

## Phuket: beaches and islands

Phuket is the easy resort part of the trip. Patong is lively and busy, while Kata, Karon and Kamala are calmer, and the quiet west coast beaches are better for families. Phuket Old Town has colourful Sino-Portuguese shophouses, cafes and weekend food markets, and is worth an evening.

From Phuket you can take a boat day trip to the Phi Phi islands, or to Phang Nga Bay with its limestone cliffs. Seas can be rough in the monsoon months, so boat trips may be cancelled or restricted. Always listen to boat crew on life jackets and conditions.

> **Tip:** Keep one day of your Phuket stay free as a weather buffer. If a boat trip is cancelled, you can reschedule it without losing your flight home.

## When to go

The cooler, drier months from roughly November to February are the most comfortable and the busiest. Hotter weather builds from March to May, and the monsoon brings rain from around June to October, particularly on the Andaman coast around Phuket. Northern Thailand has its own seasons, and burning season haze can affect Chiang Mai in the early part of the year. Plan around these broad patterns and check current conditions before you book.

## Practical points

- Visa and entry rules for Indian passport holders can change, so please check the current requirements on the official source or with us.
- Carry some Thai baht for markets and small shops, and check your card's overseas charges.
- Keep luggage light, as domestic flights have their own baggage limits.
- Dress modestly at temples and remove shoes where asked.
- Travel insurance is advisable for any trip with island activities.
