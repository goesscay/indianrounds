Travel insurance is a policy that helps with specific costs if something goes wrong on a trip, such as a medical emergency, lost baggage or a cancelled flight. It does not cover everything, so the most useful thing you can do is read what your particular policy covers, what it excludes and how to make a claim before you travel.

This article explains the usual features in general terms. It does not recommend any insurer, and every policy is different, so always rely on the policy document and the insurer's own explanation.

## What travel insurance typically covers

Policies vary, but many include some combination of the following.

- **Medical expenses:** treatment, hospital stays and emergency care abroad, up to a stated limit
- **Emergency evacuation:** transport to a suitable hospital or back home in serious cases, if the policy includes it
- **Baggage loss or delay:** compensation for lost, stolen or delayed bags, usually with limits per item
- **Passport loss:** help with costs of replacing a lost passport abroad
- **Trip delay or cancellation:** costs linked to specified reasons, such as illness, within the policy's terms
- **Personal liability:** cover if you accidentally cause injury or damage to someone else
- **Accidental death or disability:** a fixed benefit under defined conditions

No single policy automatically includes every item. Compare what is in your plan, not what is in a friend's.

## Why medical cover matters most

Medical care abroad can be expensive, and your Indian health insurance may not cover treatment outside India, or may cover it only partly. Check whether your existing health policy has any overseas cover, and do not assume.

![River Rafting on the Zanskar, Ladakh](/photos/places/ladakh/zanskar-river-rafting.jpg)

When comparing policies, look at the overall medical limit and whether it suits the country you are visiting. Some destinations have higher healthcare costs than others, and some countries ask for a minimum level of cover as part of entry or visa requirements, so check the official source for your destination.

Also check whether the policy pays the hospital directly (often called cashless) or whether you pay first and claim later. This affects how much money you need to have available during an emergency.

## Read the fine print

The headline benefits are easy to find. The details are where policies differ, and these are the parts worth reading slowly.

1. **Exclusions:** situations the policy will not pay for, such as certain activities, events or circumstances
2. **Pre-existing conditions:** whether health conditions you already have are covered, limited or excluded, and what you must declare
3. **Adventure activities:** whether trekking, scuba diving, skiing, paragliding or similar activities are covered, and up to what altitude or depth
4. **Age limits and loading:** how age affects eligibility and terms
5. **Deductibles and co-payments:** the part you pay yourself before the insurer pays
6. **Sub-limits:** smaller caps within a larger benefit, for example a limit on one item of baggage or on room rent
7. **Waiting periods and start dates:** when cover begins and whether it applies to bookings made before you bought the policy

Declare everything honestly when you buy. An incomplete declaration can lead to a claim being reduced or refused.

## Cancellation cover and its conditions

Trip cancellation cover usually applies only to reasons listed in the policy. A change of mind is typically not one of them, though some policies offer optional upgrades. Read the list of accepted reasons, and keep in mind that the insurer will want evidence.

![Nathu La, Sikkim](/photos/places/sikkim/nathu-la-pass.jpg)

Buying soon after you book your trip often gives you the widest cover for cancellation reasons, because many policies are linked to when the booking was made. Check the timing rules in your policy.

> **Tip:** Save a PDF of your policy, the insurer's emergency helpline and your policy number in your phone, and share them with a family member. In an emergency you will not want to search for them.

## How claims usually work

The process differs by insurer, but a typical claim follows a pattern.

- Contact the insurer's helpline as early as you can, especially for hospital admissions
- Keep every document: bills, receipts, discharge summaries, boarding passes and booking confirmations
- For theft or loss, report it to the local police and get a written report
- For airline delays or lost baggage, ask the airline for a written confirmation of the delay or a property irregularity report
- Submit the claim within the time the policy allows, with all the original documents requested

Write down what happened and when, while it is fresh. The more complete your records, the easier the claim.

## Choosing a policy that fits your trip

Think about the shape of your trip. A weekend break within India has different risks from a month abroad, and a relaxed beach holiday differs from a mountain trek. Match the cover to the activities and the destination, not just the price.

Consider whether you need a single-trip policy or an annual multi-trip one if you travel often. Compare the medical limits, exclusions and claim process of two or three options. A slightly higher cost can be worthwhile if it removes an exclusion that matters to you, such as cover for an adventure activity you plan to do.

Credit cards or travel packages sometimes include limited insurance benefits. Check what they actually cover rather than assuming they replace a full policy.

## Before you buy

Ask yourself what could realistically go wrong on your trip and whether the policy would help. Read the policy wording, not just the brochure, and ask the insurer to explain anything unclear in writing.
