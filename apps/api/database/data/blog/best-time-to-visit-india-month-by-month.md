There is no single best month for India, because the country has several climates at once. As a rule, the plains, deserts and south are most comfortable from November to February, the Himalayan hills shine from March to June, and the monsoon months suit green, quiet places such as Kerala and the Western Ghats, rather than high mountain roads.

Weather varies every year, so treat the months below as broad patterns and check conditions close to your dates.

## How India's seasons work

India has roughly three seasons for travellers. **Winter** (November to February) is cool and dry across the north and pleasant in the south. **Summer** (March to June) is hot in the plains and ideal for the hills. The **monsoon** (roughly June to September) brings heavy rain to most regions, arriving first in the south and west.

Two regions break the pattern. Parts of Tamil Nadu and the southeast coast get much of their rain in the north-east monsoon, around October to December. High-altitude regions such as Ladakh and Spiti are mostly accessible in the warmer months, because winter snow closes roads.

## Month by month

- **January:** Cold in the north, with fog on the plains. Good for Rajasthan, Goa, Kerala and the Andamans. Snow in Kashmir and the upper Himalaya.
- **February:** One of the best months for Rajasthan, Kerala and Goa. Hill stations are still chilly.
- **March:** Warming up. Good for Sikkim, Darjeeling and the lower Himalaya as flowers bloom. Rajasthan heats up late in the month.
- **April:** Hill stations in Himachal and Uttarakhand fill up. Plains are getting hot. The Kashmir valley is in spring.
- **May:** Peak summer break for the hills. Ladakh roads typically open around this time, but conditions vary.
- **June:** The south-west monsoon starts in Kerala and moves north. Ladakh is busy and the hills still work early in the month.
- **July:** Heavy rain in the west and across the Himalaya. Ladakh and Spiti are drier than most hill regions, while landslides are a concern elsewhere.
- **August:** Green landscapes in the Western Ghats and Kerala. Be flexible with plans in hill states.
- **September:** The monsoon eases. A good time for Ladakh and for the first rain-washed greenery in Rajasthan.
- **October:** A strong all-rounder, with clear skies in the hills and the festival season starting. Book early.
- **November:** Excellent for Rajasthan, Gujarat, Goa, Kerala and the Andaman Islands.
- **December:** Peak season for beaches and the south. Snow trips in Kashmir and Himachal, with higher prices around Christmas and New Year.

**November to February:** Rajasthan's forts and deserts, Kerala's backwaters, Goa's beaches and the Andaman Islands are all comfortable. **July to September:** Choose carefully. Kerala's monsoon is popular for Ayurveda and quiet backwaters, Ladakh works in its dry season, and the Western Ghats turn green. Avoid long road trips in the central Himalaya and the north-east, where landslides are common.

## The north-east and the islands

Meghalaya and Sikkim are best from October to April, with a second window in spring for flowers in Sikkim. Their monsoon is heavy. The Andaman Islands are generally best in the drier months, roughly November to April, while the sea can be rough and ferries unreliable in the rains.

## Shoulder months can be smart

October and March sit between seasons. Crowds are smaller and prices can be lower than at peak times, though weather is less predictable. For flexible travellers, these months often give the best balance.

> **Tip:** Big holidays such as Diwali, Christmas, New Year and the May to June school break drive demand and prices up. If your dates are fixed, book flights and stays several weeks ahead.

## Planning around your own calendar
