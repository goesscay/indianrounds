Choose Manali if you want the classic mountain holiday with snow points and adventure, Kasol if you want a quiet river valley with easy walks, and Dharamshala (with McLeod Ganj) if you prefer monasteries, cafés and mountain views. Most people have time for two of the three, and that is a better trip than a rushed run through all of them.

The three bases are far apart by road, so choosing well matters more than ticking every name.

## Manali: the all-rounder

Manali, in the Kullu Valley, is the easiest base for first-timers and families. It has plenty of hotels, restaurants and activities, and it is a gateway to higher places.

![Atal Tunnel Lahaul](/photos/places/himachal-pradesh/atal-tunnel-lahaul.jpg)

- **Old Manali** has a laid-back café scene, riverside walks and small guesthouses.
- **Hadimba Temple** sits in a deodar forest and is a pleasant short visit.
- **Solang Valley** is the usual stop for paragliding, ropeway rides and, in winter, snow play.
- **Atal Tunnel** and the road into **Lahaul** open up a different, drier landscape for day trips.

Manali can be crowded in peak May to June and around long weekends, so book ahead and start days early. Rohtang Pass and similar high places have rules and permits that change, so check the current position before planning around them.

## Kasol and the Parvati Valley

Kasol is a small village on the Parvati River, about three to four hours from Bhuntar airport depending on traffic. It attracts backpackers, trekkers and people who want slow days by the water.

Typical things to do:

- Walk to **Chalal**, a short, quiet riverside trail.
- Visit **Manikaran**, known for its hot springs and gurdwara.
- Try a day hike toward **Tosh** or **Malana**, keeping in mind that Malana has local customs for visitors, so follow what residents ask.
- Trek the **Kheerganga** route if you are fit and have time, usually as an overnight outing.

Facilities are simpler than in Manali, and mobile networks can be patchy. This is the best choice for slow travel, but less suited to travellers with mobility concerns or very young children.

## Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj

Dharamshala is a district of two halves. The lower town has government offices and a cricket stadium. **McLeod Ganj**, higher up, is the part most visitors come for, with a strong Tibetan presence and views of the **Dhauladhar** range.

- The **Tsuglagkhang** complex and the nearby Tibetan museum give context to the community.
- **Bhagsu** has a small waterfall and a temple, and is a short walk or drive.
- **Triund** is a popular day trek, often done as an overnight camp.
- **Dalai Lama temple** area cafés serve momos, thukpa and Tibetan bread.

For Dharamshala, the nearest airport is at Gaggal, which has limited flights, so many travellers fly to Chandigarh or take the train to Pathankot and drive on. Check current schedules, as they change.

> **Tip:** Treat mountain driving times as rough estimates. Landslides, traffic and weather can add hours, so keep your last day flexible and do not schedule a flight on the day you leave the hills.

## How far apart are they?

Manali to Kasol is roughly a half-day by road, and Manali to Dharamshala is a long day on the road, so plan a full travel day. Dharamshala to Kasol is not a quick hop either. If you pick two bases, choose either Manali and Kasol, which sit in the same broad region, or Dharamshala and Manali with a full travel day in between.

## When to go

March to June is pleasant in most places, with spring blossoms and open roads. July to mid-September is monsoon, when landslides and road closures are common, so be flexible or skip it. October and November are clear and crisp. December to February brings snow to higher areas, which many travellers want, though some roads close and nights are cold.

## Easy ways to combine

1. Manali (3 nights) and Kasol (2 nights), good for mixed groups.
2. Dharamshala (3 nights) and Amritsar or Chandigarh before or after for a city break.
3. Manali (4 nights) with a Lahaul day trip, if you like road journeys.

Add a night or two in Shimla on the way if you enjoy colonial-era hill-station walks and the toy train from Kalka, which runs on a scenic narrow-gauge line.
