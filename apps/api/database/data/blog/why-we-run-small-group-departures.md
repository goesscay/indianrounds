We keep our groups small because small groups travel better. Dinners are easier to arrange, plans can change without a fuss, and everyone gets a bit more attention. This piece explains how that works in practice and what it means for you, especially if you are travelling alone.

## What "small group" means for how a trip runs

A group departure is a fixed-date trip where several travellers join the same itinerary. A small group simply means we plan around a manageable number of people, so the trip does not turn into a crowd moving from stop to stop.

Practical things get easier. A coach is not blocking narrow streets, a restaurant can seat everyone together, and a guide can be heard without a microphone. The aim is a trip that feels like a shared journey rather than a procession.

Each trip page on [group tours](/group-tours) shows its own itinerary and details, so you can see how a particular departure is set up before you decide.

## A tour manager on the trip

Every group departure travels with a tour manager, called the trip captain on our trip pages. That person's job is to look after the plan on the ground: coordinating transfers, checking in at hotels, handling the small problems that tend to appear on any trip, and making sure nobody is left wondering what happens next.

![Sighnaghi, Georgia](/photos/places/georgia/sighnaghi.jpg)

This matters most when something changes. A closed road, a late flight or sudden rain can reshuffle a day. Having one person who knows the whole itinerary, and who can talk to drivers and hotels, keeps the trip calm.

## Dinners and daily decisions

Food is where group size shows most clearly. In a large group, dinner often means a set menu at a fixed place because nothing else can seat everyone. In a small group, the tour manager can book a table somewhere with good local food, ask about vegetarian or other preferences, and make a different choice if the first place is full.

The same goes for small daily decisions. A short stop for a view, a walk through a market, or an earlier start to beat the heat are all simpler with fewer people to coordinate.

## When plans need to change

Travel rarely goes exactly to script. Weather, road conditions and local events can all affect a day. With a small group, changes are easier to explain and quicker to carry out.

![Kolsai Lakes, Kazakhstan](/photos/places/kazakhstan/kolsai-lakes.jpg)

Here is how that usually helps:

- **Re-ordering the day.** If a sight is crowded or closed, the group can visit something else first and return later.
- **Handling delays.** A smaller group can be moved faster through check-ins and transfers.
- **Listening to feedback.** If most people would like more time somewhere, there is room to adjust within what is practical.

Not every change is possible, and some depend on bookings and local conditions, but a smaller group gives the tour manager more options.

## Solo travellers are welcome

Many people travel alone on our departures. Some are first-time travellers, some are experienced, and some simply have friends who cannot take the same dates. A small group is a comfortable setting for this, because it is easy to get to know people over a few meals.

If you are travelling solo, check each trip page for how rooms are arranged and any single-supplement details, since these differ between trips. Our guide to [solo travel from India](/blog/solo-travel-from-india-first-timers) covers more of what to think about before your first trip.

> **Tip:** Join the first dinner even if you are tired. It is the easiest way to meet your group, and the rest of the trip feels warmer afterwards.

## What a small group does not mean

We want to be clear about what this is and is not. A small group does not mean a private trip. You still share the itinerary, the vehicles and many meals with other travellers, and the schedule is fixed in advance.

If you would rather travel with only your own family or friends, that is a different kind of trip. Our [custom trips](/custom-trips) team plans private itineraries around your dates, pace and budget.

## Choosing a departure that suits you

When you look at a departure, check these points:

1. The dates and duration, and whether they suit your leave.
2. The itinerary, to see how much is planned and how much is free time.
3. The inclusions and exclusions, so you know what is and is not covered.
4. Any notes on pace or physical effort.
5. The terms shown on that trip.

If something is unclear, ask before you book. We would much rather answer a question than have you arrive with the wrong expectations.

Small groups will not suit everyone, but for many travellers they strike a good balance between the ease of a planned trip and the feel of a personal one. Whether you pick a long international route or a short weekend, a well-run small group makes the days simpler.
