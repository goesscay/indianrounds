Pondicherry is one of the easiest weekend trips from Chennai and Bengaluru: a small coastal town with a French-colonial quarter, quiet lanes, good cafés and a calm sea. Two nights is enough to see the main sights without hurrying, and this is how we would spend them.

Treat the plan as a template. It works best at a slow pace, with long breaks for food and walks.

## Getting there

From Chennai, Pondicherry is roughly a three to four hour drive along the East Coast Road, with views of the sea and several stops on the way. Buses and taxis are available, and the drive is the most flexible option if you want to stop at the beach or a café. From Bengaluru, it is a longer journey of around six to seven hours by road, so it suits a Friday night departure better than a Saturday morning start.

The nearest train stations are at Villupuram and Pondicherry itself, with limited services, so check what runs on your dates. Pondicherry has a small airport, but flights are limited.

## Friday evening: settle in

Aim to arrive by evening and check in to a stay in or near the **French Quarter**, also called White Town. Small heritage guesthouses here are in restored houses with courtyards, and staying here means you can walk to most things.

Take a first evening walk along the **Promenade** by the sea. Many people stroll, cycle or sit on the rocks. The area is traffic-restricted at certain hours, so check local timings.

## Saturday: the French Quarter and town

Start early, before the heat builds. Wander the grid of streets with yellow and pastel buildings, bougainvillea and tree-lined lanes. Walk past the colonial buildings and churches, and stop at a bakery for a croissant or a cup of filter coffee.

Between the quarter and the bazaar, you will find the **Tamil Quarter**, with a different pace. Here you can see traditional houses, small shops and the **Manakula Vinayagar Temple**. The **Sri Aurobindo Ashram** area is a quiet, respectful space, and photography rules apply, so follow signs.

For lunch, try a mix of cuisines. Pondicherry is known for French-influenced cafés, but also for Tamil meals and seafood. Dishes worth looking for include fish curry, crab preparations, and simple French-style crepes and baguette sandwiches.

> **Tip:** Rent a bicycle or a scooter for the Quarter if you are comfortable with it, and carry ID. Parking is tight in peak season, and walking or cycling is often faster.

## Saturday afternoon and evening: beaches and shopping

After a rest, head to **Paradise Beach**, reached by a short boat ride from the Chunnambar jetty south of town, or choose one of the quieter beaches along the coast. Water conditions vary, so swim only where locals do and respect any red flags.

In the evening, browse boutique shops for handmade paper, linen clothes, incense and home decor. Many of these are produced locally, and prices vary, so look around before you buy. Dinner can be at a seafront café or a courtyard restaurant in the Quarter.

## Sunday: Auroville and onward

Drive to **Auroville**, an experimental township a short distance north of the city. The **Matrimandir**, a large golden sphere, is its best-known landmark. Visiting the inner chamber usually requires booking and following rules, so check the current process and timings on the official channels first. The township also has cafés, craft shops and bakeries run by residents.

Return to town for a late lunch, then start your drive home by mid-afternoon to avoid evening traffic on the highway.

## A simple checklist

- Book a stay in or near the French Quarter for the walkable layout.
- Carry cash for small shops, since not all of them take cards.
- Wear light cotton, sunscreen and comfortable shoes.
- Keep a modest dress style in religious and ashram areas.
- Book restaurants early on long weekends.

## When to go

October to March is the most pleasant season, with cooler evenings. April to June is hot and humid. The north-east monsoon brings rain, mainly from October to December, so keep an umbrella and a flexible plan. Long weekends and festival periods are busy, and prices tend to rise.

Pondicherry is easy for couples, friends and families, and works well for a first road trip.
