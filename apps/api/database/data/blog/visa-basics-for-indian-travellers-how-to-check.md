The only reliable way to know whether you need a visa is to check the official source for your destination, using your nationality, your passport and your travel dates. Visa rules differ by country, change without much notice, and sometimes depend on the purpose and length of your stay, so a rule you heard from a friend or read in an old blog post should never be your final answer.

This guide does not list country-by-country rules. Instead, it shows you how to find the current answer yourself, and what to prepare once you know it.

## Start with your passport

Before you look at any country, look at your own passport. Check the expiry date and the number of blank pages. Many countries expect your passport to be valid for a certain period beyond your travel dates, and some expect a minimum number of blank pages for stamps. The exact requirement varies, so read it on the official site rather than assuming.

If your passport is close to expiry or nearly full, apply for a renewal early. Passport processing can take time, and you do not want it to delay a trip you have already booked.

## Find the official source

Search for the embassy or consulate of the destination country in India, or the official immigration or foreign affairs website of the country itself. Official government sites usually end in a government domain and do not ask for money just to read information.

![Registan Square, Samarkand](/photos/places/uzbekistan/registan-square.jpg)

Be careful with look-alike websites. Some third-party sites copy official pages and charge extra fees, or collect your personal data. If a site asks you to pay before it has told you which visa you need, step back and find the official page.

Also check the airline. Airlines are often responsible for checking your documents before boarding and can refuse to carry you if something is missing, so their travel-document guidance is a useful second check.

## Common visa types in general terms

Names differ from country to country, but most fall into a few broad groups.

- **Visa required before travel:** you apply in advance, often with a form, documents and sometimes an appointment or interview
- **E-visa or electronic authorisation:** you apply online and receive an approval, which you carry with you
- **Visa on arrival:** you get the visa at the border, under conditions set by that country
- **Visa-free entry:** you can enter for a limited stay without a visa, under conditions
- **Transit visa:** needed in some cases if you change flights in a country and leave the airport or stay beyond a set time

Which category applies to Indian passport holders depends on the destination and can change, so never assume. Even where a visa is not needed, entry conditions such as return tickets, accommodation proof and funds can still apply.

## Documents you are usually asked for

Requirements differ, but these items come up again and again.

![Baku Boulevard](/photos/places/azerbaijan/baku-boulevard.jpg)

1. A valid passport, with the validity and blank pages the country asks for
2. A completed application form, filled accurately
3. Recent passport-size photographs that meet the stated size and background rules
4. Confirmed return or onward tickets
5. Hotel bookings or an invitation letter showing where you will stay
6. Proof of funds, such as bank statements, to show you can support your trip
7. Proof of ties to India, such as an employment letter, leave approval or business documents
8. Travel insurance, if the country requires it

Names and dates on every document should match your passport exactly. A small spelling mismatch is one of the most common reasons for delays.

## Timing: how early to apply

Processing times vary widely, from a few days to several weeks, and they can be longer in busy seasons. Check the stated processing time on the official site and add a comfortable margin for mistakes, courier time and holidays.

A sensible habit is to confirm your visa requirement as soon as your dates are fixed, and to avoid paying for non-refundable flights or hotels until you are confident about the process. If your plan is flexible, ask about refund and change terms before you book.

> **Tip:** Create a folder on your phone and in your email with scans of your passport, visa approval, tickets and hotel bookings. Carry printed copies too, since some airports and borders still ask for paper.

## What happens at the border

A visa, where required, permits you to travel to a country and apply for entry, but the final decision is made by the immigration officer. Be ready to answer simple questions about your trip, show your bookings, and explain how long you plan to stay. Keep your answers short, honest and consistent with your documents.

Do not overstay, and do not use a visa for a different purpose than the one you applied for. Rules on working, studying or staying longer differ from tourism, and breaking them can affect future applications.

## Special cases and putting it together

Travelling with children may require extra documents, and some destinations ask for health documents or vaccination records, so check official guidance for your route. If you have had a visa refused before, read the instructions carefully and consider asking the embassy directly. On a group trip, ask the organiser which documents they need and by when, but a valid passport and visa remain your responsibility.

Once you know what is required, write a checklist with a deadline for each item and work backwards from departure.
